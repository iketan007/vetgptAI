# Basic Pet First Aid

## Before anything else
First aid is meant to stabilize a pet on the way to a vet, not replace
veterinary treatment. Keep a phone number for your regular vet and the
nearest 24-hour emergency clinic saved in advance.

## Minor cuts
Gently clean with saline or lukewarm water, apply light pressure with
a clean cloth to control bleeding, and keep the pet from licking the
area. Seek vet care if bleeding doesn't stop within a few minutes or
the cut is deep.

## Choking
If a pet is pawing at its mouth, drooling heavily, or struggling to
breathe, carefully check the mouth for a visible object only if it's
safe to do so — avoid pushing an object further down. Get to a vet
immediately if the airway seems blocked.

## Suspected poisoning
If you suspect your pet ate something toxic (chocolate, a human
medication, a toxic plant, etc.), contact a pet poison control hotline
or your vet immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless specifically
instructed by a professional, as some substances cause more damage
coming back up.

## Heatstroke
Signs include heavy panting, drooling, weakness, and bright red gums.
Move the pet to a cool area, offer small amounts of water, and apply
cool (not ice-cold) wet towels to the belly and paws while heading to
a vet right away.
