# Recognizing Common Illness Signs

## General red flags
Lethargy lasting more than a day, repeated vomiting or diarrhea,
refusal to eat for over 24 hours, difficulty breathing, or sudden
collapse all warrant prompt veterinary attention.

## Digestive upset
Occasional mild vomiting or soft stool can happen from diet changes,
but blood in vomit/stool, black tarry stool, or repeated vomiting
within a few hours are emergencies. Bland, vet-approved diets (like
boiled chicken and rice) are sometimes recommended short-term, but
always confirm with a vet first, especially for pets with existing
conditions.

## Skin issues
Excessive scratching, hair loss, redness, or scabbing can stem from
allergies (food, environmental, or flea-related), parasites, or
infections. A vet visit helps pinpoint the cause since treatments
differ significantly.

## Urinary issues
Straining to urinate, frequent small urinations, or blood in urine —
especially in male cats — can indicate a urinary blockage, which is a
life-threatening emergency requiring immediate veterinary care.

## When it's an emergency
Difficulty breathing, suspected poisoning, seizures, inability to
urinate, a bloated/distended abdomen, or major trauma should go
straight to an emergency vet clinic rather than waiting for a routine
appointment.
