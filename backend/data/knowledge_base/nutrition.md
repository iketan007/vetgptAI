# Pet Nutrition Basics

## Dogs
Adult dogs generally do well on two measured meals a day. Puppies under
6 months typically need 3-4 smaller meals. Look for a food labeled
"complete and balanced" by AAFCO for the dog's life stage (puppy, adult,
senior). Avoid feeding table scraps high in fat or seasoning.

## Cats
Cats are obligate carnivores and need diets high in animal protein.
Free-feeding dry food is common, but portion-controlled wet food helps
manage weight and urinary health. Fresh water should always be available;
many cats prefer moving water (fountains) over still bowls.

## Foods to avoid
Chocolate, grapes/raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol (found in sugar-free
gum and some peanut butters), and alcohol are toxic to both dogs and
cats. Cooked bones can splinter and cause internal injury.

## Weight management
A healthy weight means you can feel (but not see) the ribs, and the pet
has a visible waist from above. Gradual weight loss (1-2% of body weight
per week) is safer than rapid diets. Consult a vet before changing a
senior or medically-managed pet's diet.
